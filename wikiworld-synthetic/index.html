Pretraining tasks where we know how much each modality matters. Each row mixes table columns, a short text and a photo, and the R² a model loses without the table, the text or the photo is fixed before any data exists. One task: the facts are split across the three modalities

In a task, a few facts drive the target and each one is shown in exactly one place. How they combine sets the necessity δ of each modality: the R² lost when it is hidden, computed from the facts alone.

Real CarMax pilot, 9 tasks of 1,000 rows (FLUX 3 Klein photos, Gemma 4 texts, DINOv3 and e5 embeddings). Each point is one modality (table, text or photo) in one task. Horizontal: the R² we designed to be lost when that modality is hidden. Vertical: the R² a learner actually loses, R²(all) − R²(without it). On the diagonal, the necessity came out as designed; below it, the learner gets less out of that modality than it should.

Where this comes from

Generated by the tfm_taskgen project (designed-necessity task generator). Worlds are compiled from Wikipedia articles; training and evaluation tasks never share a world, and WikiWorld-Real entities are excluded from generation.

What δ means

δm is the R² a model loses when modality m is hidden. Unique: each modality carries its own share. Synergistic: facts matter only in pairs, so hiding any one modality removes most of the signal. Redundant: one fact shown everywhere, so hiding any single modality costs nothing.
